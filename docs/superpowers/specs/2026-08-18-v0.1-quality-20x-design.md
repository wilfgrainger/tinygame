# TinyWorld V0.1 — 20x Quality Pass Design

**Status:** Owner-approved implementation design  
**Date:** 2026-08-18  
**Branch:** `feat/v0.1-quality-20x`  
**Base:** merged V0.1 foundation on `main` (`80951318`)

## Goal

Make the existing TinyWorld V0.1 vertical slice feel dramatically more authored, tactile, coherent and enjoyable on a real Android phone **without expanding feature scope**.

“20x” is a quality multiplier, not a feature-count target. The pass succeeds when the same world and same core activities feel substantially better to move through, look at and interact with.

## Non-goals

Do not add:

- new jobs, shops, props, vehicles or broad roleplay systems;
- multiplayer, friends, chat, trading or co-op;
- missions, economy, portals, pets or combat;
- new persistence domains;
- R2, Durable Objects or another backend;
- a second Android gameplay implementation;
- large external asset packs that break the current lightweight/browser-first model.

Existing non-core roleplay texture may remain if it does not hurt clarity, performance or originality.

## Quality pillars

### 1. Authored world composition

The player should understand the world from silhouettes and routes rather than UI instructions.

Requirements:

- Village Square remains the visual anchor.
- Woodland becomes denser and more layered near paths, with clear openings rather than evenly scattered trees.
- Mountain Rise reads as a destination from spawn and has a deliberate ascent route.
- Harbour reads as a distinct waterfront space, not merely water beside land.
- Home Lane feels domestic and warm rather than another collection of boxes.
- Roads, paths and scenery follow terrain instead of flattening it visually.
- Long sightlines terminate in landmarks, terrain, vegetation or water rather than empty horizon.

### 2. Route density and micro-discovery

Every major route should provide visual change roughly every few seconds of walking.

Use existing systems only: terrain, vegetation, props, architecture, sound, particles and discovery triggers. Prefer clusters and authored scenes over uniform scattering.

Examples of quality improvements:

- woodland archways, clearings and edge vegetation;
- summit lookout composition and stronger reward presentation using existing discovery state;
- harbour mooring composition around Tiny Raft;
- home porch/garden/lighting detail;
- benches, signs and small set dressing used as route punctuation rather than filler.

### 3. Camera feel

The camera must feel deliberately tuned for a third-person mobile game.

Requirements:

- smooth yaw/pitch response with no abrupt snapping;
- spring/damping on follow movement rather than rigid teleport-follow;
- sensible pitch limits;
- camera distance and target height keep the player readable on a phone;
- movement direction and camera orientation remain predictable;
- no camera system may require pointer lock on mobile.

### 4. Movement feel

Traversal must feel responsive without feeling digital or slippery.

Requirements:

- retain existing slope/step safety rules;
- introduce controlled acceleration/deceleration where appropriate rather than instant full-speed transitions;
- preserve direct response at low input values;
- avoid animation/physics mismatch where the avatar appears to skate;
- jumping and landing should have readable motion feedback without full rigid-body physics.

### 5. Tiny Bike and Tiny Raft physicality

These are V0.1 hero interactions.

Tiny Bike:

- acceleration and steering should feel progressive;
- visible lean should communicate steering;
- wheels rotate from travelled distance;
- mount/dismount remains direct and reliable;
- terrain limits remain stricter than walking;
- feedback should make speed readable without a permanent HUD gauge.

Tiny Raft:

- low-frequency bob/roll/pitch should make it feel on water;
- steering should be progressive rather than pivoting unnaturally;
- subtle wake/ripple feedback should appear while moving using lightweight pooled effects;
- the raft remains bounded to water and easy to recover from.

### 6. Living-world presentation

Existing villagers and atmosphere should feel intentional rather than decorative CPU work.

Requirements:

- villagers should have subtle idle motion and readable silhouettes;
- proximity reactions should not spam bubbles/UI;
- ambient motion should be sparse, coherent and cheap;
- particles should be pooled/reused where practical rather than continuously allocating entities;
- no ambient effect may compromise the 30 FPS acceptance target.

### 7. Visual coherence

Use a restrained TinyWorld palette and stronger hierarchy.

Requirements:

- fewer competing saturated colours in one view;
- consistent warm village materials, cooler woodland/water accents and clear interactable highlights;
- landmark silhouettes must dominate over decorative props;
- UI should not become a second scenery layer;
- preserve an original TinyWorld identity. Do not use other game names as durable visual terminology.

### 8. Mobile performance discipline

The 20x pass must improve perceived quality without turning the phone into a benchmark furnace.

Requirements:

- keep the existing distribution budget guard passing;
- target >=30 sustained FPS on the acceptance Android device;
- do not increase shadow-map resolution above the current setting;
- prefer fewer stronger shadow casters over many weak ones;
- pool transient effects where practical;
- avoid per-frame object creation in core update loops;
- no per-frame network/API traffic;
- any quality effect that materially hurts the Android target is removed or simplified.

## Architecture approach

The quality pass should improve focused modules rather than further enlarging `GameApp.ts` or `WorldBuilder.ts`.

Introduce small pure/configuration modules where they reduce coupling, for example:

- camera tuning helpers;
- movement smoothing helpers;
- zone composition data;
- lightweight pooled visual feedback helpers.

Do not perform unrelated large refactors. Existing public contracts remain stable unless a quality requirement genuinely needs a small extension.

## Acceptance tests

Automated checks should prove invariants, not attempt to prove beauty.

Add focused tests for:

1. movement acceleration/deceleration boundaries;
2. camera damping/pitch limits as pure math where possible;
3. bike steering/lean or response calculations;
4. raft bob/steering/wake activation calculations;
5. no regression of road terrain following and traversability tests;
6. Android touch controls remain inside the landscape viewport;
7. full typecheck, source guard, unit/Worker suite, build and distribution budget remain green.

Human acceptance remains mandatory for visual quality and fun.

## Human acceptance route

On the exact deployed candidate:

1. spawn in Village Square and identify at least three destinations without reading instructions;
2. walk to Home Lane and enter the home;
3. travel through Woodland and report whether it feels dense/authored rather than scattered;
4. climb Mountain Rise using the intended route and reach a satisfying summit/viewpoint;
5. ride Tiny Bike from town toward another zone and back;
6. reach Harbour, board Tiny Raft and steer it comfortably;
7. play for at least ten minutes on Android landscape;
8. record average/perceived frame-rate quality and any hitching;
9. family testers independently name three enjoyable things they chose to do.

A family verdict of “still looks unfinished/building-site-like” is a FAIL regardless of CI.

## Definition of done

The pass is complete only when:

- all automated checks pass on the exact branch candidate;
- no new feature family was introduced;
- `progress.md` records exact branch/deployed candidate SHAs separately;
- Android acceptance is performed on the exact deployed SHA;
- family acceptance is recorded;
- world, movement, camera, bike and raft are judged materially better than merged V0.1 by human playtest.
